The Japan Gap

Why is it so hard to sell
a foreign product in Japan?

The playbook that worked everywhere else stops working here. How decisions get made, how trust is earned, how a deal actually moves forward — all of it runs on different rules.

VS

Selling elsewhere GLOBAL STYLE

  • One champion can decide, and the deal moves quickly
  • Cold email and outbound alone can open real conversations
  • Features and ROI drive the evaluation
  • First call to pricing to signature, in a straight line
  • Contracts stay transactional and trials start immediately

Selling in Japan JAPAN STYLE

  • Approval flows through ringi and several departments — there is no single decision-maker
  • Without an established relationship you rarely get the meeting at all
  • Track record, peace of mind and depth of support drive the evaluation
  • Deals advance in stages, on a foundation of patiently built trust
  • Attentive follow-up in Japanese is the baseline expectation

REASON 01

“A great product sells itself” doesn't apply here

Japanese buyers care far more about who is selling and who else has already adopted it than about the feature list. An unknown foreign product often never makes the shortlist, no matter how good it is.

Ringi and decision by consensus

Winning over your champion is only the beginning. Only a sales team that can map and shepherd the whole approval chain — their manager, the other departments involved, IT and legal — ever gets to a signature.
